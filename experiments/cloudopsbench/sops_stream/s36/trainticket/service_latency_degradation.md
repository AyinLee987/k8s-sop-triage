# SOP: service_latency_degradation

Symptom: One or more TrainTicket services show sustained latency degradation or throughput drop.

Signals:
- alerts report LATENCY_DEGRADATION for TrainTicket services
- p50 or p90 latency increases sharply compared with normal baseline
- throughput drops for one latency candidate service
- multiple services may show degraded latency at the same time; these may be propagation from a downstream root rather than independent faults
- affected pods may still appear Running and Ready
- node-level network delay may affect several pods on the same node
- co-location of a delayed service with its callers is propagation evidence, not by itself proof of node-level network delay
- pod_network_delay and code_artificial_delay can both leave pods healthy with no application errors, so handler-duration and outbound-call latency evidence is required to distinguish them
- alerts may include TRAFFIC_ANOMALY such as an inbound SURGE; this can be a primary load driver or retry amplification from an already slow service
- alerts may include RESOURCE_SATURATION such as CPU CFS throttle on the dominant service or a downstream dependency; a fast server-side handler log does not exclude CPU scheduling or queueing delay
- pod_network_delay, node_network_delay, pod_cpu_overload, code_busy_loop, code_artificial_delay, and downstream dependency delay can all leave pods healthy with no application errors, so handler-duration, outbound-call latency, and CPU-throttle evidence are required to distinguish them
- a CPU CFS throttle alert on a downstream service in the dominant caller's call path is stronger root evidence than a larger latency spike on a separate leaf service
- a CPU-throttled service may be a transitive downstream dependency rather than a direct downstream; trace through intermediate services before excluding it
- a dominant service can have parallel downstream branches; a CPU-throttled dependency on one branch does not by itself explain latency alerts on another branch
- parallel-branch latency may be retry amplification from the slow dominant call or a separate independent candidate; record which
- fast server-side handler logs on a leaf service do not by themselves rule in pod_network_delay or rule out a CPU-throttled call-path dependency
- CPU CFS throttle can be caused by external pod-level CPU stress or by application code doing excessive work; throttle evidence alone does not distinguish pod_cpu_overload from code_busy_loop
- application logs showing repeated business-method invocations, large result-set processing, or tight-loop work under CPU throttling point to code_busy_loop rather than external pod_cpu_overload
- code_busy_loop requires both application busy-processing evidence and slow caller-observed outbound latency to the CPU-throttled dependency; busy logs plus CPU throttle alone leave code_busy_loop pending
- a parallel-branch latency alert on a service with fast server-side handler logs is not by itself pod_network_delay; if the same slow dominant caller can generate retry amplification, treat the branch as possible propagation until caller-observed outbound latency to that branch is slow and retry or load is excluded
- do not emit a secondary pod_network_delay prediction for a parallel branch merely because its handlers are fast; an unexplained fast-handler branch should be recorded as unresolved or possible retry amplification unless caller-side outbound latency confirms it
- when the dominant latency candidate is a leaf service with no downstream calls, healthy pods, and fast entry logs, caller-observed outbound latency to that leaf and server-side handler completion duration are required before selecting pod_network_delay; same-timestamp entry logs are not handler-duration evidence
- co-located affected services that form a single call chain are not independent node-wide evidence; do not emit node_network_delay as a top prediction without a degraded co-located workload outside the call chain
- a p90 roughly twice p50 on a leaf with fast handlers is network-delay-compatible, but caller-side outbound latency must still be slow
- if caller-side outbound latency to a leaf is normal, do not confirm pod_network_delay; keep code_artificial_delay or another branch unresolved

Start at: performance.interpret_latency_degradation_alerts

## Steps

### Step: performance.interpret_latency_degradation_alerts
Goal: Inspect performance alerts to identify whether latency degradation has a dominant anomalous service or a widespread propagation scope, and select a root candidate using service dependency direction rather than relative latency magnitude alone. When several services alert, upstream callers of a delayed downstream service are propagated symptoms; prefer the deepest downstream candidate that can explain the caller alerts. Record which alerted services are outside the selected candidate's call chain, because those are the only independent evidence for a node-wide fault. Also record any TRAFFIC_ANOMALY inbound surge and any RESOURCE_SATURATION or CPU CFS throttle in the selected service's call chain. A CPU CFS throttle on a downstream call-path service is a stronger root candidate than a separate leaf service with a larger latency spike; keep the CPU-throttled dependency as the preferred candidate until caller-side outbound-call latency to that dependency is shown to be normal. Trace the full transitive downstream call path from the dominant service to any CPU-throttled service; do not exclude a CPU-throttled service merely because it is not a direct downstream. Map parallel branches as well: if a CPU-throttled dependency lies on one branch while a separate leaf latency alert lies on another branch, do not select the CPU-throttled dependency until caller-side outbound latency to it is slow and the parallel branch is either explained by retry amplification or shared load or recorded as a separate candidate. If a parallel branch has fast server-side handler logs, do not convert it into a pod_network_delay prediction unless caller-side outbound latency to that branch is slow and retry amplification from the slow dominant caller is excluded; otherwise record it as possible retry-amplification propagation or an unresolved independent candidate. If the CPU-throttled dependency is on the call path, also treat application busy processing in that dependency as a possible code_busy_loop source to be tested from logs, but keep code_busy_loop pending until caller-observed outbound latency to that dependency is slow. If the selected candidate is a leaf service, mark caller_outbound_latency_to_leaf pending and do not default to pod_network_delay from same-timestamp entry logs alone.

### Step: performance.inspect_cpu_saturation_and_traffic_surge_candidates
Goal: Resolve whether load-driven CPU saturation, a CPU-throttled downstream dependency, or application code busy processing explains the latency before attributing to network delay. Inspect CPU CFS throttle alerts and pod placement for the dominant service and its downstream dependencies. If a downstream dependency is CPU-throttled, use the dominant service's recent logs to extract caller-observed outbound call duration to that dependency, for example call start and completion timestamps or request and response gaps, and compare it with the dependency's own handler duration. If the CPU-throttled dependency is not a direct downstream, trace through intermediate services, for example ts-travel-service -> ts-seat-service -> ts-order-service, and use the immediate caller's logs to extract outbound call duration to the throttled service. Also inspect the throttled dependency's logs for repeated business-method invocations, large result-set processing, or tight-loop work that could indicate code_busy_loop rather than external pod CPU stress. Treat an inbound surge on the dominant service as possible retry amplification, not automatic proof of primary load. For any latency alert on a parallel branch, record whether the alert can be explained by retry amplification from the dominant service or remains an independent candidate. If the parallel branch has fast server-side handler logs, extract caller-side outbound latency to that branch before ranking pod_network_delay; if that latency is not slow, record possible retry amplification and do not default to pod_network_delay. If the dominant candidate is a leaf service, inspect the immediate caller's logs for outbound call start and completion to that leaf and compare it with the leaf's handler completion duration; same-timestamp entry logs on the leaf are not handler-duration evidence. Do not confirm pod_network_delay or prefer an unrelated leaf-service latency spike while CPU saturation on the call path or an unexplained inbound surge remains unresolved. Do not discard a parallel-branch latency alert merely because a CPU-throttled service on another branch has been identified.

### Step: performance.confirm_pod_cpu_overload_or_code_busy_loop
Goal: Confirm CPU-related latency when a CPU CFS throttle exists on a service in the latency call path and the caller's outbound calls to that service are slow or show scheduling or queue delay, even if the throttled service's own handler logs show fast processing once running. Then distinguish the CPU saturation source: if the throttled service logs show application-level excessive or repeated work, such as repeated getSoldTickets calls, large result-set processing, or tight-loop behavior, classify code_busy_loop. If CPU stress is external to application logic and no such busy-code evidence exists, classify pod_cpu_overload. Do not label pod_cpu_overload when application busy processing explains CPU consumption. Confirm code_busy_loop only when application busy-processing logs are present in the throttled service and caller-observed outbound latency to that service is slow. If caller-observed outbound latency to the throttled service was not obtained, do not confirm code_busy_loop solely from busy logs; keep it as the primary candidate with confirmation pending and record the missing evidence. Before concluding, explicitly account for all dominant latency alerts: if an alerted service is not downstream of the throttled service and is not explained by retry amplification or shared load, keep that branch as an independent candidate rather than attributing the whole incident to code_busy_loop. For a parallel branch with fast server-side handler logs, if caller-side outbound latency to that branch is not slow, record it as possible retry amplification from the slow dominant caller rather than ranking it as pod_network_delay; only rank pod_network_delay when caller-observed outbound latency to that branch is slow and retry or shared load is excluded. If caller-side outbound calls to the throttled service are normal, record the CPU throttle as unrelated or excluded for this incident and proceed to other latency candidates. If slow, confirm the CPU-related cause and do not attribute the same latency to pod_network_delay.

### Step: performance.exclude_node_network_delay
Goal: Decide whether the latency pattern is consistent with node-level network delay. If affected pods are spread across nodes, pod-local network delay remains more likely. If they share a node, node_network_delay is only a candidate until an unrelated co-located workload is also shown degraded; co-location of the dominant service with its own callers is not sufficient because callers inherit the callee's latency. Also exclude CPU saturation on the shared node as the explanation. If the only shared-node services are in one call chain and no independent co-located workload is degraded, record node_network_delay as excluded or conditional rather than emitting it as a top prediction.

### Step: performance.verify_independent_node_wide_impact
Goal: Distinguish node_network_delay from pod_network_delay by checking whether at least one service on the shared node that is not in the dominant service's call path also shows unexplained latency or network degradation. Co-location of the dominant service with its direct callers is propagation evidence, not independent node-wide evidence. CPU saturation or other non-network anomalies on the node may be recorded but do not by themselves confirm node network delay. If no independent non-call-chain service is degraded, exclude node_network_delay rather than ranking it as a secondary prediction solely from co-location.

### Step: performance.exclude_latency_application_errors
Goal: Exclude application-level errors by inspecting error logs for the anomalous downstream service selected from latency alerts. Also inspect recent logs to establish preliminary handler-duration and outbound-dependency-latency context, without yet making the final pod_network_delay attribution. If recent logs for the dominant service cannot be retrieved, do not treat the failed retrieval as evidence that there is no application-internal delay; retry with the current container logs or use an alternative log or metric source, and record the uncertainty. If CPU throttling is present, also use these logs to preserve preliminary evidence of application busy processing that could indicate code_busy_loop.

### Step: performance.attribute_pod_network_delay_by_exclusion
Goal: Confirm root cause pod_network_delay only after the CPU-saturation and traffic-surge step has excluded a load-driven or CPU-throttled downstream candidate and has also excluded application code_busy_loop as the CPU-saturation source, and after comparing three latency views for the dominant service: caller-observed latency, server-side request-handler duration and internal processing gaps, and duration of the pod's outbound calls to its dependencies. Pod_network_delay is supported when caller-observed latency is high while server-side handler duration is short, outbound calls to dependencies are not materially slower than normal, pods are healthy, application errors are excluded, independent node-wide impact is absent, and no unresolved CPU CFS throttle, code_busy_loop, unexplained parallel-branch latency alert, or inbound surge explains the latency. A separate leaf service with a larger latency spike but fast server-side handler duration is not by itself enough to choose pod_network_delay while any CPU-throttled or busy-code call-path dependency remains unverified. Do not attribute pod_network_delay to a parallel or leaf branch solely from fast server-side handler logs; require slow caller-observed outbound latency to that branch and exclusion of retry amplification from a confirmed or primary code_busy_loop dependency. For a leaf service, require a caller log that shows the outbound call start and completion and demonstrates slow caller-observed latency; same-timestamp leaf entry logs are not handler completion and do not establish fast handler duration. If caller-observed outbound latency to the leaf is normal, do not confirm pod_network_delay and record code_artificial_delay or another branch as unresolved. A p90 roughly twice p50 is network-delay-compatible but is not sufficient without caller-side slow outbound latency. Prefer code_artificial_delay when server-side handler duration or internal gaps are long despite fast dependencies. Prefer code_busy_loop when application logs show repeated business-method work or large result-set processing under CPU throttling and caller-observed outbound latency to the throttled dependency is slow. Prefer a downstream dependency or downstream network delay when outbound calls from the dominant service are slow or dependency logs show slowness. If recent logs are unavailable, state the limitation and keep pod_network_delay conditional rather than overclaiming; do not use failed log retrieval to confirm node_network_delay or pod_network_delay. Do not emit node_network_delay as a top prediction unless an independent co-located non-call-chain workload is degraded. The terminal root cause label emitted here is pod_network_delay only when the comparison supports it.
